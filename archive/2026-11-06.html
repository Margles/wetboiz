    Can $n_s$ hint at a parity-odd sector of gravity?

    Christian Dioguardi

    Classifies the poles of the kinetic function induced by integrating out the distortion in projectively invariant metric-affine gravity with a non-minimally coupled inflaton. It shows that third-order poles, which give n_s about 1-3/(2N) and r proportional to N^-3/2, require parity-odd operators.

    Parity-violating scalar-tensor theory revisited: complete basis up to $d=4$ and Hamiltonian analysis

    Yun-Chuan Zhang, Yu-Min Hu, Xian Gao

    The paper builds complete bases of parity-violating scalar-tensor and spatially covariant gravity monomials up to derivative grade 4 and finds six integration-by-parts relations. A Hamiltonian analysis shows the lapse-velocity term propagates four degrees of freedom and the extrinsic-curvature-velocity term six, leaving six covariant polynomials (one d=3, five d=4) with three degrees of freedom.

    Parity-violating scalar-tensor theory Hamiltonian analysis; matches wanted paper.

    Asymptotic safety is not generic

    Francesco Del Porro, Francesco Ferrarin, Alessia Platania

    The authors derive FRG beta functions for SU(2) and SU(3) chiral models at four-derivative order and find no interacting fixed point. They argue that asymptotic safety is not generic and point to the volume term and ghost contributions as features gravity has and the chiral models lack.

    arXiv:2611.07002hep-thgr-qcrelevance 8quality 6

    Asymptotic safety genericity via FRG; title closely matches a wanted paper.

  4. 4

    Demystifying Graviton Detection

    Igor Pikovski

    The paper explains why single-graviton detection, long thought hopeless with atomic absorption, may be feasible by absorbing a graviton into a phonon of a macroscopic quantum resonator, whose larger mass quadrupole raises the cross-section by many orders of magnitude. It also argues that detection would be genuine particle detection but not final proof of quantized gravity.

    arXiv:2611.07447gr-qchep-thquant-phrelevance 8quality 7

    Graviton detection; matches wanted paper of same title.

    Testing Gravitationally-Induced-Entanglement with Massive Particles and Quantum Clocks

    Germain Tobar, Markus Aspelmeyer, Magdalena Zych

    The paper proposes testing gravitationally induced entanglement between a levitated massive particle and a large array of nuclear clocks, where the clocks' internal energy contributes to active gravitational mass. It estimates decoherence times that drop to hours or days for 10^23 scandium clocks, and gives dynamics and scaling derivations in the appendices.

    arXiv:2611.08313quant-phgr-qccross-listrelevance 7quality 7

    Proposal testing gravitationally induced entanglement with massive particles and clocks.

  6. 6

    Improving Gravitationally Induced Entanglement in Interferometric Configurations

    Alireza Maleki

    The paper derives gravitational entanglement phases for five Stern-Gerlach and Mach-Zehnder interferometer geometries and optimizes their geometric ratios. It finds that parallel-arm designs give the largest phase and are most robust to fabrication errors, and that unoptimized geometries can lose several orders of magnitude in signal.

    arXiv:2611.07153quant-phgr-qchep-thcross-listrelevance 7quality 6

    Gravitationally induced entanglement interferometer optimization; tests of quantum gravity.

  7. 7

    Viable Phantom Crossing in Softly Broken Shift Symmetric Gravity

    Rodrigo Calderon, Eric V. Linder

    Proposes a Horndeski model with softly broken shift symmetry (exponential field dependence in the kinetic and braiding terms, no potential) that crosses w=-1 near z~0.5. It fits BAO, supernova, and CMB data better than w0waCDM by Delta chi^2 of about -3 with the same number of parameters.

    arXiv:2611.07035astro-ph.COgr-qcrelevance 6quality 7

    Horndeski shift-symmetric modified gravity with phantom crossing; fit to data.

  8. 8

    Dynamical and Cosmological Perspectives on Symmetric Teleparallel Gravity and its Extensions

    Pooja Vishwakarma

    A PhD thesis studying late-time cosmic acceleration in symmetric teleparallel f(Q), f(Q,B) and f(Q,phi) gravity. It uses critical-point stability analysis, BBN constraints and Noether symmetries, and reports models with stable late-time dark-energy attractors and exact solutions.

    Symmetric teleparallel modified gravity f(Q) cosmology and dynamics.
